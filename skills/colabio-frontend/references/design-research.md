# Research basis: distinctive structure and component craft

Reviewed 2026-10-22. Sources below are award organizations and the studios that
describe the work. Studio descriptions are attributed claims, not independent
proof of performance or evidence that every live interaction was tested.
The design rules synthesized from them are editorial recommendations.

## Criteria: craft includes usefulness

[Awwwards evaluation system](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/) lists
Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, and Content 10%. It does not prescribe
a universal visual style. The skill therefore reviews concept and originality
alongside content and functioning interfaces, without predicting jury scores.

[The Webby Awards criteria](https://www.webbyawards.com/judging-criteria/) assess
content, structure/navigation, visual design, functionality, interactivity,
innovation, and overall experience. Its website guidance treats appropriate
design and usable organization as substantive qualities.

**Synthesis:** "premium" is not a reliable instruction by itself. A design needs
a particular organizing idea and a useful experience, backed by rendered work.

## Oura: a learning journey, not a feature-card catalog

[Instrument's Oura case study](https://www.instrument.com/work/oura-smart-ring)
describes audience research, revised information architecture, progressive
disclosure, and a combination of product UI, data visualization, lifestyle
imagery, and CGI. It explicitly balances distinct experiences with reusable
components. The case-study page and its content hierarchy were also inspected
in a browser; this review did not independently test the Oura commerce journey.

**Transferable principle:** connect explanation and interaction to the visitor's
learning task. Reveal relevant depth without overwhelming the first view.
**Do not transfer:** Oura's brand, imagery, ring metaphor, or page layout.

## Desktop Metal: domain knowledge generates component anatomy

[BASIC/DEPT's Desktop Metal case study](https://www.basicagency.com/case-studies/desktop-metal-website-redesign)
describes information architecture derived from customer/sales interactions,
technical references for the visual language, intention-based navigation,
contextual product education, and a view of printed parts. Its modules support
both scanning and deeper investigation.

**Transferable principle:** the project's actual objects, user questions, and
evidence can produce distinctive components and navigation structures.
**Do not transfer:** its technical aesthetic or 3D presentation to unrelated work.

## Chartogne-Taillet: place and heritage become the interface

[Immersive Garden's Chartogne-Taillet case study](https://immersive-g.com/projects/chartogne-taillet-1)
describes vineyard exploration through hand-drawn maps, historic map references,
watercolor treatments, and animated landscapes. The documented interactions
connect plots to their histories. These are studio descriptions; live vineyard
navigation and device performance were not independently measured here.

**Transferable principle:** choose a visual subject and interaction model rooted
in the actual place, collection, or process.
**Do not transfer:** watercolor effects or map exploration as a universal luxury recipe.

## Orano: interaction demonstrates the subject

[Immersive Garden's Orano case study](https://immersive-g.com/projects/orano)
describes stylized wireframe tools and interactive exploration to explain field
technology. The representation and input mechanisms serve the technical subject.
The studio reports the recognitions listed on its page; no new award assessment
was made during this research.

**Transferable principle:** make the focal interaction demonstrate what matters
about the product, using an appropriate representation rather than decorative realism.
**Do not transfer:** game-like controls into tasks that require direct, efficient input.

## Webisoft: identity extends through a complete system

[Locomotive's Webisoft case study](https://locomotive.ca/en/work/webisoft)
shows a digital rebrand expressed through a logo, graphic/3D assets, motion,
website screens, and mobile presentation. Its description connects the work
to the client's technical identity and navigation goals.

**Transferable principle:** coherence across assets, components, and breakpoints
contributes to distinction. One polished hero cannot supply that coherence alone.
**Do not transfer:** futuristic visuals or 3D logos as mandatory elements.

## Resulting changes to Colabio Frontend

The skill now requires an experience concept, a content-derived page plan,
a working signature component, a rendered vertical slice, connected visual
language, and separate reviews of originality and usability. These are a
synthesis of the research and the earlier Haven demo's observed limitations.

The previous Haven variants retained the same basic hero/process/property/
calculator/FAQ/CTA structure while changing fonts, colors, and animation. That
demonstrated visual refinement, not proof of distinctive page architecture.
The updated skill no longer treats that kind of reskin as sufficient evidence
of a substantive creative redesign. No new website was built in this research pass.
