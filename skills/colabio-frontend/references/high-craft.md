# High-craft page architecture and components

Read this for a new premium site, a full landing page, or a substantial creative
redesign. Apply the relevant parts to the brief; do not impose this whole process
on a small patch. This playbook is a design method, not a layout template.

## 1. Form an idea that can produce a structure

Start from a concrete product truth, user tension, process, material, place,
or collection. A useful idea generates choices beyond a color palette.

Weak: "A premium property landing page with animated sections."
Stronger: "Follow one property from first impression to an inspectable rental
scenario, keeping the assumptions and unresolved questions visible."

The stronger idea suggests a persistent subject and connected evidence. It
does not require a specific palette, a giant serif headline, or a scroll effect.
Write what a visitor should understand, feel, and be able to do by the end.

For an open full-page brief, compare two viable concepts before selecting one.
Describe the difference in the experience, not just its mood. For example, an
object-led investigation and a comparison-led decision tool are distinct logics.
Choose against the task, available content, implementation scope, and audience.

## 2. Research mechanisms, not fashionable surfaces

When research is useful and browsing is available, inspect a small, relevant
set of references across more than one studio or product. Include at least one
reference that handles the same information problem, rather than only visually
spectacular sites. Respect user instructions that prohibit browsing.

For each reference, record:

- Which visitor need its structure serves.
- How content is grouped, revealed, compared, and navigated.
- Its dominant visual subject and the parts that make it specific.
- What the user actually does, including the mobile/static alternative if observed.
- The transferable principle, what should not transfer, and the source URL.

Distinguish direct observation from a studio's description. Do not claim to have
tested a live interaction because a case study mentions it. A reference board
is input to original design, not permission to reproduce another site's assets,
recognizable layout, proprietary illustrations, or brand identity.

## 3. Plan the page at two scales

At the whole-page scale, decide the opening, evidence sequence, focal interaction,
points of rest, and next action. Label scenes by their purpose, not by a mandatory
catalog of sections. Useful planning fields are:

| Scene | Visitor question | Evidence/content | Composition | Interaction | Connection forward |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Project-specific name | What must become clear? | What substantiates it? | How does the eye move? | What changes or becomes inspectable? | Why continue? |

Do not fill the plan with generic labels and call it a concept. Delete a scene
whose purpose another scene already fulfills. Show the product or its actual
subject early enough to ground the promise. Put proof next to the claim it
supports instead of isolating all credibility at the bottom.

At the component scale, decide the anatomy of the focal experience. Specify
which elements persist, which change, what comparison is possible, and what
feedback makes the result understandable. A composition can be quiet and still
be particular: a carefully annotated object may communicate more than WebGL.

## 4. Design a signature component as an information instrument

Choose a mechanism only when it solves the brief. These examples illustrate
relationships, not required component types:

| Context | Domain-specific possibility | Why it may earn its place |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property research | Linked property annotation, assumptions, and cost waterfall | A changed assumption visibly affects a decision rather than only changing a headline number. |
| Architecture | Plan/section/image correspondence with selectable annotations | Visitors understand spatial relationships that a detached image carousel cannot explain. |
| Technical product | Inspectable assembly linked to feature evidence | Details connect capabilities to the physical product. |
| Craft or hospitality | Material, location, or process exploration with contextual stories | The place and making process organize the experience. |
| Service business | A navigable case investigation with decisions and documented outcomes | Expertise becomes inspectable instead of an interchangeable list of capabilities. |
| Data software | A constrained working scenario with linked views and an explicit conclusion | Users experience the product's reasoning and limits. |

For the chosen component, record a compact contract:

- **Purpose:** the question it answers and the useful action it enables.
- **Anatomy:** spatial relationships, dominant object, hierarchy, and affordances.
- **Content:** real labels, representative data, assets, and honest demo boundaries.
- **States:** default, selected, changed, expanded, interrupted, and applicable failures.
- **Input:** pointer, keyboard, touch, and an equivalent simple route to the information.
- **Motion:** triggers, continuity, timing, property ownership, and cancellation.
- **Adaptation:** what is reordered or simplified on a small screen and without motion.
- **Proof:** the behavior and rendered details that demonstrate completion.

Then implement it with reliable primitives. The component's information model,
composition, and feedback distinguish it, not the number of custom hooks or shaders.
Do not manufacture graphs, map coordinates, calculated risk scores, or unavailable
product capabilities to make the display look richer.

## 5. Create continuity without monotony

Make major scenes behave like connected arguments. A selected object can persist
as its context changes; a diagram can become a comparison; a decision can lead
to relevant evidence. Use these relationships only when they clarify the story.

Vary dominant composition where the content warrants it: a broad image, an
annotated detail, a dense evidence view, or a quiet statement can have different
roles. Do not turn this into an obligation to include one of each. Shared type,
grid logic, iconography, annotation style, and media treatment hold the site together.

Design a visual motif from the project's subject and reuse it deliberately in
navigation, captions, data, or transitions. A motif is not a repeated floating
sticker. It should establish recognition and help orient the visitor.

## 6. Art-direct the assets and the details

Identify the indispensable images, diagrams, product states, or footage before
finalizing layout. Specify content, crop, focal point, treatment, placement, and
its relationship to type. Set an asset budget appropriate to the project.
If assets are missing, prefer an honest, useful diagram or working product
view over an unrelated decorative photo. Use licensed or user-owned material.

In the rendered vertical slice, inspect:

- Optical alignment, image crop, and spacing between different shapes.
- Heading breaks, punctuation, type weight, and paragraph measure with real text.
- Control geometry, label spacing, icon stroke, and focus/selected treatment.
- Motion start and end states, interruption, reverse actions, and layout stability.
- Mobile reading order, useful crop changes, long content, and static fallbacks.

Carry the same decisions into secondary routes and states that are in scope.
Reusable components are desirable; an unmodified component-gallery aesthetic
is not a substitute for art direction.

## 7. Diagnose a weak result before adding polish

| Symptom | Likely design problem | Useful correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attractive but interchangeable | No product-specific concept or anatomy | Rework the focal component and information relationships. |
| Long stack of repeated blocks | Section list substituted for a narrative | Reorder by visitor questions, remove duplicates, vary purposeful compositions. |
| Memorable first screen, bland remainder | The hero was designed in isolation | Extend the visual language through evidence, transitions, and the ending. |
| Motion-heavy but generic | Effects were applied after a stock layout | Design what persists, changes, and becomes clearer before selecting animation. |
| Looks sophisticated only in a screenshot | States and interaction were not designed | Exercise the real task, interrupted states, and narrow layout. |
| Highly original but confusing | Novelty weakened navigation or feedback | Restore familiar affordances and a direct route to the content. |

Use qualitative judgments with concrete observations. Do not convert a checklist
into an invented award score. A full-page screenshot, a working focal interaction,
and completed usability checks provide evidence; they do not guarantee an award.
