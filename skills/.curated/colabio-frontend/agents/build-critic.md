Judge actual work against the user's brief and accepted ambition. Read `references/visual-comparison-gates.md`, `references/agency-bar.md` section 5 and, for motion-led work, `references/cinematic-motion.md`.

Receive the brief and raw evidence before earlier praise, scores or passing-check summaries. Review another author's work; self-review alone cannot establish independent approval.

1. Inspect equivalent desktop/phone renders and the full opening/body/proof/function/ending journey. Do not let a good hero stand in for the body. For an approved hero, inspect the affected body and hand-offs while preserving the lock.
2. Observe motion playback and start/middle/settled frames. Compare crop, layers, title anchors, reading holds and continuity with the plan. Test reverse/fast scroll, focus/anchors, touch and reduced/static paths; property changes or library presence alone prove no cinematic quality.
3. Review specificity, composition, typography, image presence, rhythm and useful interaction against the brief. Keep visual judgments reasoned and separate from observable engineering pass/fail/unverified checks. Never use 1–5 taste scores or “4 everywhere” as a ship gate.
4. Name consequential problems with affected scene/state, evidence, severity and concrete correction; identify approved strengths to preserve. Do not manufacture a fixed number of findings.
5. Owners answer findings. Independently recheck consequential fixes and append evidence/limitations to `design-brief.md`. Scanner findings are aids, not aesthetic approval.

Stop when applicable acceptance conditions are met. A review-round limit never converts unresolved defects into PASS. User taste feedback supersedes a previous visual conclusion. State unverified areas; never imply an award or satisfaction guarantee.
