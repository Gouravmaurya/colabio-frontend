On our desk,and in our heads.

  • Turn-based rooms where your past runs are the only teammates you get. The room at the top of this page is the first two. Planned next: more rooms, a level editor, and co-op where two players’ clones overlap.

  • A cable-laying puzzle on a tidal map. The water level is the clock, and your earlier routes stay wired for the next tide.

  • A small management game about one factory run by the same crew in shifts, who leave each other notes instead of talking.

A good room is a sentence you can hold in your head. We build them small, make them cheap to retry, and keep every system honest: a clone does exactly what you did, and nothing more.

Five rules we make games by. We’ll add to the list when a prototype teaches us something.

  1. R1

    A room fits on one screen.

    If you have to scroll to understand the puzzle, the puzzle is too big. Split it.

  2. R2

    Failing costs one key press.

    Undo and restart are always one input away. People bring bolder ideas to a room they can reset instantly.

  3. R3

    Clones never cheat.

    A replay is deterministic. If a clone fails, the reason is on the board and the player can see it.

  4. R4

    A level is a sentence.

    “Hold the plate while you walk through the door.” If we can’t say it in one line, we don’t ship it.

  5. R5

    Prototype before polish.

    Squares first. Art arrives once the mechanic has survived a stranger sitting down with it.
