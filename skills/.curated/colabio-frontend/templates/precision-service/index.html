Parts people
stop finding.

Five kinds of job we take most often. Open a row for what to expect.

  • Oven, cooker and washer dials with a D-shaft or splined bore. Send the old knob or the shaft diameter and flat position. We print in ASA or PETG when the knob sits near heat, then test-fit the bore.

  • Mixer, blind-motor and toy gears. Nylon is the first choice for teeth that mesh; we print at a fine layer height so tooth flanks stay smooth. Tell us the module or tooth count if you know it.

  • Parts that flex or snap. Layers run along the part, not across it, so the print orientation matters more than the layer height. We choose it and tell you why.

  • Shelf, sensor, camera and bike-light brackets. Load decides infill and wall count, so we ask what hangs from it. Screw holes are printed slightly undersized and drilled when fit matters.

  • Small rings that take up play or damp vibration. Quick, cheap and easy to batch — ask for a set of ten with different bore sizes to find the one that fits.

Before you send.

I don’t have a CAD file. Can you still help?

Yes. Send the part, or photos with a ruler beside it and the key dimensions. We model it and send you the model to approve before printing. Modelling is quoted separately.

How accurate are the parts?

Plan on about ±0.2 mm for ordinary desktop printing, tighter on small features when we tune for it. Tell us which dimensions are fit-critical and we’ll print those with allowance and test-fit them.

Which file types do you accept?

STL, 3MF, STEP and OBJ. STEP is best for parts that need tight fits because it keeps true curves and holes.

What if it doesn’t fit?

Send the measurement that’s off. One revision reprint is included in the original price; further revisions are priced like a new job.
